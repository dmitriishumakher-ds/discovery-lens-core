INTERVIEWER: Yemi, thanks for joining. Tell me a bit about your role and how you use Notion.

YEMI: So I do all of our internal and external comms — company blog, newsletter, social media, internal announcements, some PR. Notion is where we keep everything — editorial calendar, content briefs, brand guidelines, draft documents, the lot. I've been using it for two years since I joined.

I work from home but I'm also constantly moving around. I do school runs, I'm out a lot, I travel a fair bit for company events and customer visits. So I'm probably fifty-fifty between laptop and phone on any given day.

INTERVIEWER: Let's start with what works. What do you genuinely value about Notion?

YEMI: The editorial calendar is really good. Being able to see all our content by status, by publication date, by platform, by who's assigned — in one database view — that's massively better than the spreadsheet we had before. And the ability to link a content brief to its calendar entry, and link the calendar entry to a campaign, and have everything visible from one record — that kind of relationship structure is genuinely powerful.

Also, collaboration is fine. Comments work. Mentions work. My manager and I go back and forth on draft documents without issue. That part is solid.

INTERVIEWER: Now what doesn't work?

YEMI: [exhales] Mobile. Can I talk about mobile for a while?

INTERVIEWER: Please do.

YEMI: So here's a day I had last month that is representative of the problem. I was at my son's school thing in the morning, and our head of marketing sent me a message saying a piece we'd planned had been bumped up by two weeks and I needed to update the editorial calendar and brief the freelancer. Fine, normal stuff.

I open Notion on my phone. I navigate to the editorial calendar — that takes a bit because the sidebar navigation on mobile is awkward, you have to swipe and scroll. I find the content item. I try to change the publication date. That requires opening the record and editing a date field. On desktop this is two clicks. On mobile I'm tapping, the date picker comes up, it's a tiny wheel scroll thing that's hard to control precisely on a small screen. I change the date. I try to add a comment to brief the freelancer. The comment box is at the bottom, but the keyboard covers it when it opens and the page doesn't scroll up the way I expect, so I end up typing without seeing what I'm typing. I post the comment. Something has gone wrong — the formatting is off, there's a line break in the middle of a sentence.

This whole thing took maybe six or seven minutes on mobile. On desktop it would have been forty-five seconds. And those minutes were frustrating minutes, not productive minutes.

INTERVIEWER: Is that experience typical?

YEMI: It's typical for anything that involves creating or editing content. Reading is okay. If someone shares a link with me and I just need to read it — fine, works on mobile. But anything involving editing, creating records, writing comments, using databases — it's consistently worse than desktop. Not slightly worse. Significantly worse.

And the thing is — for my job, mobile capture is actually important. I'm often away from my desk when something time-sensitive comes up. If I can't deal with it on my phone, I either have to wait until I'm at my laptop — which sometimes means things slip — or I deal with it badly on mobile and then fix it later on desktop. That's double handling.

INTERVIEWER: Have you tried any workarounds?

YEMI: I've tried a few. For quick notes, I use WhatsApp to message myself and then transfer to Notion later. Which is obviously ridiculous — I'm using a messaging app as a notes inbox for my notes app. I also keep a Moleskine notebook for actual quick capture when I'm in meetings or events. The notebook is more reliable than Notion mobile for that specific use case, which is a strange thing to say in 2025.

Someone in a Notion community I'm in suggested using Notion Web Clipper as a workaround for quick capture on mobile. I tried it. It's not really designed for quick notes, it's for saving web content. Doesn't help.

INTERVIEWER: Let me ask about something different — how do you find things in Notion? You have a lot of content.

YEMI: Hit and miss. If I remember the name of something, I can usually find it through search. If I only remember vaguely what something was about — "there was a document about the rebranding, what was it called" — I often can't find it through search. I end up browsing the folder structure, which works if I remember where I put it.

The thing that really frustrates me is finding content inside records. A lot of my actual work content — briefs, draft copy, notes — lives inside database records. Notion's search doesn't reliably find text inside database record bodies. So searching for a specific phrase that I know is in a brief often returns nothing, and I have to open records one by one.

INTERVIEWER: You mentioned the AI features earlier. Does Notion AI help with findability?

YEMI: I tried it. The Q&A feature where you ask Notion AI a question and it finds relevant content — I tested it a few times. It works better than keyword search for some queries. But it's an add-on cost, which we haven't approved for the team. So I see it, I can try it occasionally, but it's not in my actual workflow because it's behind a paywall.

And honestly that's a bit frustrating. The feature that would solve my main pain point — finding content across my workspace — is the one they're charging extra for on top of an already expensive subscription.

INTERVIEWER: If someone at Notion was listening to this conversation, what would you want them to know?

YEMI: That mobile isn't an edge case for me. It's half my work life. I think Notion was designed for someone sitting at a desk with a big monitor and plenty of time to set things up carefully. That's not most of my working day. And I suspect it's not most of their users' working days either, especially now that remote and hybrid work is just how work is.

If the mobile app felt as capable as the desktop — if I could edit, create, comment, and search with the same ease — I would be a much happier user. Right now I work around it. I don't want to work around it.

INTERVIEWER: Last thing — your editorial team, do they use Notion the same way you do?

YEMI: Our freelancers use it to read and comment on briefs. That works fine. My manager uses desktop mostly. I'm probably the most mobile-dependent person on the team and I feel it the most.

We've talked about whether there's a better tool for content management. Someone suggested Airtable for the editorial calendar specifically. I'm not opposed to looking. But I don't want to manage two tools — I'd rather Notion fixed the thing.

INTERVIEWER: Brilliant, thank you Yemi. Really useful.

YEMI: Hope so. Fix the mobile app. Tell them I said that.
